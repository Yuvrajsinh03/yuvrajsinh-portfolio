01 — The Brief

A platform for professional introductions needed a mark as precise as the act itself.

Yntro is a SaaS tool for high-volume connectors — people who make dozens of professional introductions a week — to track every one they've ever made. Who was introduced, when, what happened next. Your network, finally visible.

The brand brief was simple in words, hard in execution: feel like LinkedIn grew up and went independent. Trustworthy without being corporate. Warm without being casual. The product is about human connection, and the visual identity had to carry that weight from the first pixel.

02 — Discovery

Understanding what trust looks like in a professional context.

Before touching a single design tool, I needed to understand the space. Professional networking tools tend toward two failure modes: cold corporate blue that says nothing, or warm startup orange that says too much. Yntro had to thread the needle.

I audited 20+ identity systems across professional tools, networking apps, and B2B SaaS. The pattern that emerged: the most trusted brands use colour with restraint and let form carry the meaning.

Four discovery lenses shaped the brief:

01
Competitive Audit
Mapped 20 professional tools across identity traits — colour temperature, mark complexity, typeface personality, icon style. Found a clear gap: nothing in the space communicated "relationship" — they all communicated "network."
02
Semantic Mapping
Workshopped brand words with the founding team: "introduction," "bridge," "trust," "visible," "connector." These became the semantic anchors that every visual decision was measured against.
03
Symbol Research
Explored how the concept of "two people connected" has been represented historically — handshakes, bridges, arcs, nodes. Sought a form that felt instantly readable at 24px but meaningful at 300px.
04
Typeface Audition
Tested 40+ typeface pairings against the brand words. Cormorant Garamond for display (editorial authority, warmth) and DM Sans for UI (modern, readable, non-corporate) emerged as the winning pair within the first day.
03 — Design Principles

Four principles that governed every decision in the system.

1
The mark must tell the story without the wordmark
Any icon that requires text to explain itself has failed. The Yntro mark — two dots connected by an arc with reaching arms — needed to communicate "two people being introduced" at 28px without a label. The test: could someone describe it as a handshake or bridge without prompting? After 30 iterations, yes.
2
Colour carries relationship, not brand
Navy-to-blue gradient wasn't chosen because it looks like a tech company. It was chosen because the gradient literally is the introduction: Person A (navy, depth, initiator) flows into Person B (blue, energy, recipient). The brand story lives inside the colour system itself.
3
Cormorant earns its seat at the table
Most SaaS tools default to geometric sans-serifs that feel clean but lifeless. Cormorant Garamond brings editorial warmth — the kind of type you'd find in a quality business publication. It signals that Yntro takes relationships seriously, not as data points but as stories worth telling.
4
Scale gracefully — or don't scale at all
A brand identity that breaks at notification-icon size (28×28px) is not a brand identity — it's a large-format illustration. Every element of the Yntro system was tested at every size from the beginning, not as an afterthought. The simplified favicon mark was designed in parallel with the full wordmark.
"An introduction is a relationship. Treat it like one."
Brand manifesto — written as a design constraint before the first mark sketch
04 — Mark Conception

The name yntro — etymology as identity.

The brand name is a phonetic compression of "introduction" — stripping it to its kinetic core. Yntro sounds like an action, not a product. It says: something is about to happen. Someone is about to meet someone.

The mark follows the same logic. It compresses the entire concept of a professional introduction into six elements: two people (the dots), the path between them (the arc gradient), and the moment of meeting (the two arms reaching inward). Every element has a semantic job.

30+ mark sketches were explored across four directions — nodes and edges, bridge forms, handshake abstractions, and person-silhouette marks. The winning direction was the only one that worked at 24px, read instantly as "two people," and contained the gradient as meaning rather than decoration.

introduction
Latin · introductio · "a leading in"
From intro- (inward) + ducere (to lead). The act of bringing two people across a threshold to meet. The brand name collapses this to its essential form — an action, not a noun.
the mark
Visual etymology — six semantic elements
Navy dot → Person A (initiator). Blue dot → Person B (recipient). Gradient arc → the introduction in motion. Two arms → the handshake. The form is the story. Remove any element and the meaning collapses.

06 — Mark Anatomy
Person A — Connector Navy
The initiator. Dark navy carries depth and trust — the connector's authority and history behind every introduction. The person doing the introducing.
Person B — Yntro Blue
The recipient. Bright blue represents openness and possibility — the other side of the bridge, ready to be connected. The person being introduced to.
The arc — Brand Gradient
The introduction in motion. Navy flows into blue — the journey from one person to another. The gradient is the connection itself, not decorative colour.
The arms — handshake gesture
Two shapes reaching toward each other from each side. The physical act of introduction — one hand from each side, meeting across the arc.

08 — Typography

Two typefaces, each with a distinct job to do.

Cormorant Garamond brings editorial gravity to display contexts — the kind of type you'd find in a quality financial publication. It signals that Yntro takes relationships seriously, not as data but as stories.

DM Sans handles everything the product needs to say clearly and quickly — navigation, labels, body copy, CTAs. At weight 300 it's warm; at weight 500 it's authoritative. DM Mono anchors metadata to a point in time.

The pairing was tested against 40+ alternatives. This was the only combination that felt neither corporate nor startup — it sat in exactly the right middle ground that Yntro needed to occupy.

09 — Voice & Taglines

10 — App Icons

The mark at every scale — from App Store to favicon.

Most marks are designed at 500px and scaled down as an afterthought. The Yntro icon was designed in parallel at all five scales from the start. The 28×28 favicon and the 1024×1024 App Store asset were drawn in the same session, testing the same decision simultaneously.

At small sizes the simplified mark (just two dots + arc curve) is used instead of the full mark — the arms become invisible below 32px and were removed rather than corrupted.

11 — Outcome

A complete identity system, shipped in three weeks.

The brand shipped into production for Yntro's beta launch and across all marketing surfaces. The colour system and mark became the foundation for the entire product design system — every component in the app uses the same semantic colour tokens defined in this document.

The wordmark and mark have been in continuous use since 2022 without modification — a signal that the system was designed with enough inherent logic that it didn't need constant revision.

12 — Reflection
What I'd do differently — and what I wouldn't touch.
What worked
Starting with semantic constraints — "the gradient is the introduction" — before touching design tools forced every visual decision to have a reason. The mark didn't need a brief; it was the brief made visible. This approach is now how I start every identity project.
What I'd change
The three-week timeline worked because the founder trusted the process completely. I'd build in a structured critique phase with a neutral external reviewer — not to change the work, but to stress-test whether the mark read across cultural contexts I hadn't considered.
Unexpected learning
Cormorant Garamond was chosen as the display typeface partly on instinct and partly on competitive research. Unexpectedly, it became the most recognisable element of the brand to users in feedback sessions — more memorable than the mark itself in early testing. Typography is not secondary.
On brand identity
A brand identity is not a logo file and a colour hex. It's a set of decisions with reasons — reasons that survive the original designer leaving the project. The Yntro brand guidelines document exists so that anyone can design within this system without asking me. That's the actual deliverable.
